## How to Use the Caesar Cipher

1. **Choose Encode or Decode:** Decide whether you want to hide a message (Encode) or reveal a hidden message (Decode).
2. **Set the Shift:** The Caesar Cipher works by shifting the alphabet by a certain number of letters. The standard shift (historically used by Julius Caesar) is 3, but you can set this to any number between 1 and 25.
3. **Translate:** Type your text into the input box. The cipher will instantly shift every letter in your message while ignoring numbers, spaces, and punctuation.

## What is a Caesar Cipher?

The Caesar cipher is one of the simplest and most widely known encryption techniques. It is a type of substitution cipher in which each letter in the plaintext is replaced by a letter some fixed number of positions down the alphabet. 

For example, with a shift of `1`, `A` would be replaced by `B`, `B` would become `C`, and so on. When it reaches the end of the alphabet, it wraps around (so `Z` becomes `A`).

### The History of the Cipher

The cipher is named after Julius Caesar, who used it with a shift of three to protect messages of military significance. According to the Roman historian Suetonius, Caesar used this simple encryption to communicate securely with his generals.

While it was likely effective thousands of years ago, the Caesar cipher offers almost zero security today. Because there are only 25 possible ways to shift the English alphabet, anyone can break a Caesar cipher simply by trying every combination (a "brute force" attack). 

### ROT13
A very famous variation of the Caesar Cipher is **ROT13**. This simply applies a shift of exactly 13. Because the alphabet has 26 letters, applying ROT13 twice restores the original text. You can create a ROT13 cipher using our tool above simply by setting the shift amount to 13.
