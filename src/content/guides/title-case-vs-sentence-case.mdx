## What is the Difference?

When writing headlines, email subjects, or book titles, you generally have two capitalization styles to choose from: **Title Case** and **Sentence Case**.

### Sentence Case

Sentence case mimics the capitalization of a standard sentence. You capitalize the very first letter of the headline and any proper nouns (names, places, brands). Everything else is written in lowercase.

* **Example:** *The complete history of the Roman empire*

**Pros of Sentence Case:**
* It feels more conversational and casual.
* It is easier and faster to read, especially on mobile devices.
* You don't have to worry about complex grammar rules regarding which words to capitalize.

### Title Case

Title case requires capitalizing the first letter of most words in the headline. However, there are strict rules about which minor words should remain lowercase.

* **Example:** *The Complete History of the Roman Empire*

**Pros of Title Case:**
* It looks more formal, authoritative, and traditional.
* It is widely required by academic style guides (APA, MLA) and journalistic publications.

## Rules for Writing in Title Case

If you choose to use Title Case, you must follow specific rules. While different style guides (like AP Style vs. Chicago Manual of Style) have slight variations, the general consensus ([The Chicago Manual of Style Online](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/)) requires:

1. **Always capitalize the first and last word**, regardless of what part of speech they are.
2. **Capitalize all major words**: nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, and subordinating conjunctions.
3. **Lowercase all articles**: a, an, the.
4. **Lowercase coordinating conjunctions**: and, but, for, or, nor.
5. **Lowercase short prepositions**: on, in, at, to, by, with. (Note: Some style guides capitalize prepositions that are five letters or longer, like "Between" or "Through").

## Which Should You Use?

There is no objectively "correct" choice for web writing—it depends entirely on your brand's voice. 

Modern tech companies (like Apple and Google) and modern blogs heavily favor **Sentence case** because it feels approachable. Traditional publishers (like The New York Times) and academic journals strictly adhere to **Title case**. According to the [Google Developer Documentation Style Guide](https://developers.google.com/style), sentence case is strictly preferred for all headings and titles.

Whichever style you choose for your website or publication, the most important rule is **consistency**. Do not mix Title Case and Sentence Case across your blog posts.
